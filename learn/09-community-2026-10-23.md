## The winning public recipe (0.60 → 0.624)
Source: nishantdahal "Two-stage LoRA, part two" (LB 0.620, CV 0.642), and nuridudak 0.624, which adds encoders and
typography guards on top of it.
1. **The public pool.** About 84k passages from 26 community Kaggle datasets, all generated from the 18 models with prompts in the
   competition's style. It is filtered to one paragraph, 30–300 words, no markdown, no duplicates, no near-copies of train
   prompts (content TF-IDF cosine < 0.3), and no punctuation a model never uses in train.
2. **Two-stage LoRA.**
   - Stage 1 trains on the pool, stage 2 on train (2–3 epochs).
   - Base models are Qwen3-14B-Base (first 27 of 40 layers) and Qwen2.5-14B (first 32 of 48), with r16 on all linear layers.
   - The readout is the mean hidden state at 8 depths, each passed through its own LayerNorm, then a linear head.
   - The adapter is merged into the weights before inference.
3. **Members.**
   - A 4-view n-gram LinearSVC (char, word+punctuation, word *shape*, function words with content masked), fitted on
     train + pool: 0.473 alone.
   - DeBERTa-v3-large, two-stage: 0.546.
   - ModernBERT-large: 0.569.
   - ATLAS, a Qwen3-8B source-conditioned likelihood model trained on train only (arXiv 2610.19583): +0.2–0.7 points at
     10 % weight.
4. **Post-processing.**
   - **Centred twin:** cluster the test passages by topic, subtract the cluster mean from the features, and read the result
     with a second head. Weight 0.1.
   - **Sinkhorn balancing** so that every class gets an equal share of predictions.
   - **Typography guards:** hard rules, e.g. Claude and GLM never use curly apostrophes.

**The single biggest lever was data.** On the old 35k pool, four different backbones all finished at 0.569. Moving the same
Qwen3-14B to the 84k pool took it from 0.569 to 0.611. Stage 1 alone, without seeing any train passage, scores 0.572 on train.

## What does *not* work (several teams agree)
- **Hungarian assignment within each test prompt.** Many teams report +0.10 in CV and zero or negative on the LB. Our v3 gave
  +0.01. The test set has only a few passages per prompt.
- **Things that did not help:**
  - more parameters (once the pool is fixed)
  - seed or weight averaging
  - a Gemma-4-12B backbone
  - family gates on the strong recipe (they helped early on)
  - a stacker instead of fixed weights
  - more stage-2 epochs, or pool passages mixed into stage 2
  - character CNNs
  - lab → model hierarchies
- **Generic web corpora** (e.g. Oleg's), which don't transfer. This matches our own check.

## Open problems (where an edge could come from)
- **The CV–LB gap grows with the pool:** 0.593/0.592 on the old pool, 0.642/0.620 on the new one. The pool's generation
  settings differ from the host's.
- **The hardest pairs plateau** for every member:
  - glm-5.3 vs glm-5.3-flash
  - gemini-3.7-flash vs gemini-3.8-flash
  - the GPT-5.6 siblings
- **Gemini 3.7 Flash** has been routed to 3.8 since 2026-10-08, on both Google AI Studio and OpenRouter. Any *new* "3.7"
  passages may really be 3.8. One check found no sign of this in the host labels.
- **Short passages** (under 60 words) are the hardest for every model.
- **Self-training on the 4,000 unlabeled test passages** hasn't been reported by anyone. One rules question (751544) asks
  whether it's allowed, with no answer yet. It goes straight at the pool-vs-host shift.

## Rules status
- **There is still no host answer** on generated data. Threads 749613, 750038, 750512, 751011, 751294, 751544 and 751954 all
  ask about it.
- **Community practice:** everyone trains on the public CC-BY pool, and several people publish their free-quota
  generations daily.
- **Forking whole notebooks** is universal and has not been challenged.
- **Prize risk:** if the host later rules generated data out, every top solution is hit. Keep a no-pool fallback in mind.

## Engineering pitfalls reported
- **Slow `/kaggle/input` reads:** model mounts can be 20× slower. Copy the weights to `/tmp` first. Kaggle says this was
  stabilized on 10-23.
- **Qwen3-14B-Base tokenizer on Kaggle Models:** it ships the chat tokenizer, whose end token is `<|im_end|>` (151645)
  rather than `<|endoftext|>` (151643). This silently flips 14 % of predictions. Use the end token id stored at training time.
- **No bf16 on T4:** use fp16. A 14B model fits across 2×T4 with `device_map="auto"` and 13–14 GiB each.
- **Failed scoring runs** ("Notebook Threw Exception" after 3–4 h): reported since 10-21. A submission that fails still uses
  a slot.

## What this means for us
- Our pipeline (v3: TF-IDF + frozen Qwen probes, 0.439) is two generations behind. Our LoRA 0.6B (blend CV 0.472) would
  still land around 0.46.
- Our own daily free generation (about 840 passages/day, 12 labels) is small next to an 84k pool that the community grows
  every day. It is only worth it if we add something the pool lacks, e.g. **clean host-style settings** or labels
  that few datasets cover.
- The strategy update in [07-strategy.md](07-strategy.md) is waiting on the decision about which path to take
  (fork the public base + add our own edge, or rebuild the recipe ourselves).

## Follow-up: self-training simulation (src/selftrain_sim.py), negative
- **Setup.** Each held-out fold plays the test. The student is the public 4-view SVC; the teacher is our OOF blend + the
  SVC, Sinkhorn-balanced per fold.
- **Reproduction check.** The SVC on train + pool scores **0.4745**, matching the public notebook's 0.473.
- **Results:**

  | student | alone | blended with our models | pseudo-label precision |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | train only | 0.369 | 0.482 | |
  | train + pool (public member) | 0.475 | **0.527** | |
  | + top 30% pseudo per class | 0.489 | 0.523 | 0.69 |
  | + top 60% | 0.505 | 0.520 | 0.62 |
  | + all | 0.527 | 0.527 | 0.53 |

- **Reading.** Pseudo-labels make the student agree with the teacher (it scores higher alone) but add no new information,
  so the blend gets slightly *worse*. By our pre-set rule (at least +1 point in the blend) it is not worth porting.
- **Caveat.** Our teacher is weak (0.46–0.53 against the real 0.64), and we only tested the n-gram student. Self-training
  the LoRA heads on the strong ensemble's test predictions is a different, untested question.

## Follow-up: which parts of the pool help? (src/pool_source_check.py), negative
- **Setup.** The same 4-view SVC on train folds + pool, with one source or one generation setting left out, plus two
  host-likeness variants (a host-vs-pool classifier on topic-free views, fit per fold). Each is compared with the full pool
  by a paired bootstrap over prompts. Results are in `submissions/pool_source_check.csv`.
- **Every slice of the pool helps.** Leaving any source out lowers the score or leaves it flat. The biggest losses come from
  drjcfmd (-1.5, the most host-like source), lxd (-1.4) and arxitekton (-1.2). The one gain, zbigniew (+0.3), is within
  1 sd.
- **Every generation setting helps too, including the ones that don't look like the host.** Dropping "default" costs 3.7
  points, "other" 1.5, "low" 1.2, "new" 1.1 and "high" 1.0. orglm is neutral.
- **Host-likeness weighting doesn't help.** Weighting by p/(1-p) scores -0.5 and keeping the top half -1.2.
- **Reading.** For this student, volume and variety beat closeness to the host. Cleaning or reweighting the pool is not
  worth a GPU retrain of the LoRAs. The CV–LB gap is not fixed by dropping off-host data. More data is the lever, which
  argues for adding to the pool rather than filtering it.

## Follow-up: are some pool "gemini-3.7" passages really 3.8? (src/gemini37_check.py), no
- **Why we asked.** Gemini 3.7 is the worst label: recall is 0.23, and 88 of its 150 train passages go to 3.8. Since
  Oct 8, requests for 3.7 have been routed to 3.8. A train-only SVC reads the "3.7" passages from drjcfmd (setting "new")
  and prvsiyan as 3.8 at least as often as 3.7.
- **Result.** Removing them doesn't help:
  - Without drjcfmd's 375 passages: -0.1 points, and 3.7 recall *drops* from 0.23 to 0.14.
  - Without prvsiyan's 161: -0.15 points.
  - Without both: -0.2 points.
- **Reading.** drjcfmd's 3.7 passages carry real 3.7 signal, so this is not a mislabel. The 3.7/3.8 confusion is a true
  style overlap, and the fix has to come from features rather than from cleaning labels.

## Follow-up: simple style signals for the hardest pairs, none
- **What we checked.** 17 scalar features (length, word and sentence length, comma rate, and the presence of ; : — – ’ ( ! ?,
  digits, "you", asterisks, an "Imagine…" opening) for gemini-3.7/3.8, glm-5.3/flash, the gpt-5.6 siblings, and fable-5/glm-5.3.
- **Result.** Every effect size is |d| ≤ 0.4. With 150 passages per label the noise is about ±0.12, and most train effects
  don't replicate in the pool. The one consistent signal is that gpt-5.6-luna uses more em dashes and parentheses than sol
  (d about 0.2 in both).
- **Reading.** No hand rule separates these pairs. A pair specialist would need learned features, i.e. the LoRA readouts,
  not surface statistics.
